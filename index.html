A view from the field

Stop counting agents

Agents are easy to build now. Running lots of them, together and safely, is the real work. That's the job of an agentic control plane.

S
SubbuOctober 2026 · 4 min read

Every team got the same memo this year: build agents.

So they did. HR has one in Teams. Sales has one inside the CRM. The service desk built a few on a framework nobody else in the company uses. Each one works well on its own.

Now try a simple question. How many agents are running across the business today? Who owns them? What are they allowed to touch?

If the honest answer starts with "let me check", keep reading.

The next phase of agentic AI will not be defined by how many agents an enterprise can create.

It will be defined by how well those agents work together, connect to existing systems and operate with accountability.

01 · The problem

Building agents got easy. Running them didn't.

Not long ago, getting one useful agent into production was the hard part. Now agent builders are everywhere, and much of the software you already pay for ships with agents of its own.

That's progress, with a catch. Every agent holds credentials, calls systems and makes decisions. Without a shared layer above them, you get agent sprawl: plenty of capable agents, no single view, and different rules for each one.

02 · The idea

So what is a control plane?

The term comes from networking. The data plane moves the traffic. The control plane decides where traffic goes and keeps the rules.

Think of an airport. Airlines own and fly the planes. The control tower doesn't fly anything. It knows every aircraft in its airspace, decides who lands where, and keeps a record of what happened.

An agentic control plane is that tower for your agents. The agents still do the work, wherever they were built. The control plane sends each request to the right one, sets what each is allowed to do, and keeps a trace of every step.

04 · The answer

How watsonx Orchestrate approaches it

watsonx Orchestrate began as a place to build agents. It has grown into the layer that runs them, including agents it didn't build.

The part I find most useful: nothing has to be rebuilt to join. An agent built elsewhere registers without code changes and keeps running where it is. Orchestrate adds the routing, the rules and the visibility around it.

Read it left to right:

  • Left. People stay in the channels they already use.
  • Middle. Orchestrator agents decide who does the work, and AgentOps watches every step.
  • Right. Agents, tools, models and data stay where they are. Gateways and open standards bring them in.
